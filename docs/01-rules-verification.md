## Sources used (all accessed 2026-11-20)

- **[S1] Nepal Police — Kathmandu Valley Traffic Police Office, "Guidelines for Pedestrians"** (posted 2078-07-14). <https://traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np/notices/45/> — primary source for zebra/green light, parked vehicles, no-footpath walking, six-step crossing code, buses and moving vehicles.
- **[S2] Nepal Police — "Traffic signs" (Pedestrian signals section).** <https://traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np/about-us/traffic-signs/> — red human figure: do not enter the road; green: walk cautiously.
- **[S3] Nepal Police — "Traffic slogans" (ट्राफिक नाराहरु).** <https://traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np/safety-and-security/traffic-slogans/> — "Obey traffic rules", "Give way to pedestrians first", "Stop before a zebra crossing". Note: the text was seen in the site's search index; the page did not load on a direct fetch on 2026-11-20, so re-open it once before final submission.
- **[S4] Wikipedia, "Road signs in Nepal"** (citing Department of Roads, *Traffic Signs Manual*). <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_Nepal> — "Nepal drives on the left." Supporting source only: the Wikipedia page itself carries a single-source warning, so confirm with [S1]/an officer in the pitch.
- **[S5] UNICEF, "Protecting Young Lives: Global Status Report on Child and Adolescent Road Safety".** <https://www.unicef.org/reports/protecting-young-lives> — background on why child pedestrian safety matters. **Not** a source for individual rules.
- **WHO, "Road safety Nepal 2023 country profile".** <https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/road-safety-npl-2023-country-profile> — background context for the application; not a rule source.
